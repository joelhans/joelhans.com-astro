## How we ask the right questions at ngrok

Let me give a concrete example.

We're working on a video about using n8n and ngrok together. It feels very
natural to create a status quo video that starts with installing n8n, then
ngrok, then cruising through the process to wire them together. Done.

That would be a video that takes far more than it gives, because it assumes that
they already know they want ngrok.

Or want it at all.

We want to make a video that assumes no more than they have an n8n instance
running on `n8n` and want to make it accessible to the public internet. That's
it.

We can then ask very specific questions about the video to cut away the parts
where we take:

- How can we describe and empathize with their problems in ways that are not framed around our product? 
- How much of ngrok can we excise and still maintain a logical story that follows a beginning, middle, and end? 
- What does the viewer learn, and can meaningfully use, even if they stop watching just before ngrok shows up?

We answer that by giving them the best possible instruction on how to make a stable, secure, and hardened public n8n instance. We hope they'll use ngrok to make it accessible to the world.

It's more important they become a power user of solving their problem than a champion of ngrok.
